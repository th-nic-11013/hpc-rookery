# Plan: onboard PARAM Rudra into rookery

2026-11-04. Draft for approval; nothing built yet.

## What I found

- **Facts on disk:** five PARAM Rudra skills (slurm, job-scripts, gpu,
  build, performance; about 1500 lines). They cover partitions and limits,
  the account rule, Spack, the A100 GPU nodes and NUMA layout. Also your
  `rudra/` research folder: MACE fine-tuning projects and a report.
- **Access:** C-DAC drops traffic from Japanese IPs, but the work desktop
  (reached over the IITH VPN) can reach Rudra on port 4422. Its Rudra key
  **has a passphrase**, and nothing unlocks it non-interactively. So
  `BatchMode` gets "Permission denied", and rookery can't use that key
  unattended.
- **Scheduler:** Slurm, with partitions picked by job size: `debug`,
  `terai`, `shiwalik`, `himachal` and `himadri` for CPU; `gpu-debug`,
  `gpu-small`, `gpu-large` and `gpu-massive` for GPU; `hm-*` for high
  memory. Every partition has a minimum size, and every job needs
  `--account`. rookery's Slurm backend already does this; any quirks go in
  a small subclass in `facility.py`.

## Proposed access path (your one-time action)

```
Mac ──ssh (ProxyJump via work desktop)──▶ paramrudra.cdacb.in:4422
     multiplexed: you unlock once, rookery reuses the open connection
```

This is the same pattern Miyabi and Crux use: one interactive login, then
every tool call rides on it for hours.

1. Make a dedicated key on the Mac. A passphrase is fine, because
   multiplexing means you type it once per session.
2. Add its **public** half to Rudra's `~/.ssh/authorized_keys`. Do it from
   the work desktop, or through the OOD "SSH Keys" page via the SOCKS proxy
   (see the `param-rudra-remote-access` skill). Keep the existing xCAT and
   OOD keys.
3. Add a `Host rudra` block to the Mac's `~/.ssh/config`: `HostName`,
   `Port 4422`, `User`, `IdentityFile`, `ProxyJump` to the work desktop, and
   `ControlMaster auto` / `ControlPath` / `ControlPersist 8h`.
4. Run `ssh rudra` once in a terminal. rookery works until the connection
   expires.

I'll give you the exact lines when we get there. You run the key steps
yourself; I don't touch your keys or `authorized_keys`.

## Policy check (web search, 2026-11-04)

Sources: search-result summaries only. The C-DAC pages and the Rudra
manual PDF failed TLS verification, so their full text was not read.

- **There is no national NSM policy that forbids describing a system.**
  Each NSM site sets its own usage policy.
- **Acceptable use (PARAM Utkarsh, C-DAC Bengaluru):**
  - use only for the granted purpose;
  - **never share credentials or private keys**;
  - activity may be monitored;
  - violations can disable the account.
- **The PARAM Rudra User's Manual (C-DAC, 17 May 2024) is copyrighted
  and may not be reproduced without written permission.** Paraphrased facts
  are fine; copied text, tables or figures are not. PORTING §2 already
  requires a guide in our own words.
- **The manual's partition names differ from the five skills.** Other
  sites' versions use `standard`, `hm` and `gpu`. Bengaluru's partition
  list must come from the live `sinfo`.
- **Acknowledgement:** NSM sites (PARAM Yukti, Ananta, Shivay) require
  acknowledging NSM in publications and reporting them. This matters for any
  published benchmark records.
- **Hacking, reverse-engineering and IP violations** can bar a user from
  every NSM system.

Consequences for the port:
- facts only, rewritten, and checked against `sinfo`/`scontrol`;
- nothing copied from the manual;
- no access workaround;
- no credentials or personal identifiers;
- the multiplexing design keeps your key on your Mac, so nothing is
  shared.
